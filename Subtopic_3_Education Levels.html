The impact of AI adoption on Canadian Wage - Subtopic 3: Education Levels¶

How has representation in AI-related occupations changed from 2012 to 2024 across different education levels?¶

Goals

The purpose of this analysis is to investigate how different education groups are represented in AI-related jobs across Canada.

  • Load and combine Labour Force Survey (LFS) data from 2012 to 2024 for analysis.
  • Clean the data to ensure key variables (education levels, occupation, weights) are valid and usable.
  • Group education codes into meaningful categories for analysis.
  • Filter the dataset to include only AI-related occupations.
  • Calculate weighted representation of workers in AI-related occupations by education level.
  • Visualize trends over time and summarize average yearly representation to understand how education affects access to AI-related work in Canada.

Introduction

Context/Hypothesis: As AI reshapes the Canadian workforce, workers with higher education, particularly post-secondary or technical degrees, are expected to be more represented in AI-related occupations than those with lower education levels.

Problem Statement

We aim to answer the following question: How has representation in AI-related occupations changed from 2012 to 2024 across different education levels?

Significance: This research provides insights into how education impacts access to AI-related jobs in Canada, helping inform training programs, educational efforts, and workforce planning.

In this section, we load the Labour Force Survey (LFS) data from CSV files. The dataset is stored in yearly folders (2012 to 2024), and each folder contains multiple monthly CSV files.

Data Transformation: Grouping Education Levels

In this step, the detailed ISCED education codes are transformed into broader, more understandable education categories. Grouping education levels makes it easier to compare across groups and aligns with our research question on representation by educational attainment.

A new variable, EDUC_GROUP, is created by mapping ISCED codes to categories such as Less Than High School, High School, Post-Secondary Non-Tertiary, Short-Cycle Tertiary, Bachelor’s, Master’s, and Doctorate. After creating this variable, we examine how observations are distributed across education groups to understand the structure of the cleaned dataset.

In [4]:
# Map ISCED education codes to broader categories
def map_isced_to_group(isced):
    
    # Convert the value to an integer
    isced = int(isced)
    
    # Map codes to education groups
    if isced in [0, 1, 2]:
        return "Less Than High School"     
    elif isced == 3:
        return "High School"    
    elif isced == 4:
        return "Post-Secondary Non-Tertiary"      
    elif isced == 5:
        return "Short-Cycle Tertiary" 
    elif isced == 6:
        return "Bachelor's"
    elif isced == 7:
        return "Master's"       
    elif isced == 8:
        return "Doctorate"    
    elif isced == 9:
        return "Other"         
    else:
        return "Other"          

# Apply mapping

EDA and Main Analysis: Yearly Representation in AI-Related Occupations

This section combines exploratory data analysis (EDA) with a detailed analysis to examine how representation in AI-related occupations has evolved over time across different education levels. Using survey weights from the Labour Force Survey (FINALWT), we compute weighted estimates that reflect population-level employment patterns.

First, weighted totals of all employed individuals and AI-related workers are calculated for each survey year and education group. These totals are used to derive the proportion of workers employed in AI-related occupations for every education level from 2012 to 2024. The results are first explored using a table, followed by a line plot to show trends and changes over time.

In [6]:
# Create filtered datasets with positive survey weights only
lfs_pos = lfs[lfs['FINALWT'] > 0].copy()
lfs_ai_pos = lfs_ai[lfs_ai['FINALWT'] > 0].copy()

# Total weighted employment (all occupations)
total_w = (
    lfs_pos
    .groupby(['SURVYEAR', 'EDUC_GROUP'], as_index=False)['FINALWT']
    .sum()
    .rename(columns={'FINALWT': 'Total_Weighted'})
)

# Total weighted employment in AI-related occupations
ai_w = (
    lfs_ai_pos
    .groupby(['SURVYEAR', 'EDUC_GROUP'], as_index=False)['FINALWT']
    .sum()
    .rename(columns={'FINALWT': 'AI_Weighted'})
)

# Merge total employment and AI employment into a single table
yearly = pd.merge(total_w, ai_w, on=['SURVYEAR', 'EDUC_GROUP'], how='left')

# Replace missing AI values with zero (no AI employment in that group/year)
yearly['AI_Weighted'] = yearly['AI_Weighted'].fillna(0)

# Compute proportion of workers in AI-related occupations
yearly['Proportion_in_AI'] = yearly['AI_Weighted'] / yearly['Total_Weighted']

# Create a working copy used for reporting and plotting
representation = yearly.copy()

Main Findings/Conclusions

The analysis of AI-related employment from 2012 to 2024 shows clear differences in representation across education levels. The following summarizes the main trends in AI-related employment across education levels from 2012 to 2024:

  • Those with short-cycle tertiary education remain the most represented in AI occupations, averaging around 15–16% throughout the period.
  • Bachelor’s degree holders show a moderate but steady representation, with a slight increase from 12.2% to 12.6% over the years, averaging 12.4%.
  • Those with post-secondary non-tertiary and high school education experience gradual declines in AI representation, with high school graduates showing the largest drop, from roughly 11% to just under 10%.
  • Individuals with less than a high school education remain the least represented group, averaging only 6% and showing a small decline over time.

Overall, these findings indicate that AI-related employment is concentrated among individuals with higher education, particularly short-cycle tertiary and bachelor’s degrees. Representation among lower education groups has generally decreased over the past decade, highlighting the strong influence of post-secondary education on access to AI occupations.
