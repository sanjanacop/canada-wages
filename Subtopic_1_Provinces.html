The Impact of AI adoption on Canadian Wage - Subtopic 1: Provinces¶

Do wages in AI-related jobs grow differently across Canadian provinces?¶

Goals

The purpose of this analysis is to investigate wage trends in AI-related occupations across Canadian provinces between 2012 and 2024. Specifically, we aim to:

  • Examine whether provinces with larger technology sectors, such as Ontario, Quebec, Alberta, and British Columbia, experience faster wage growth in AI-intensive occupations.
  • Compare wage growth trends between urban and rural provinces to assess the potential widening of wage disparities.
  • Visualize changes over time and across provinces to support workforce development insights.

Introduction

Context/Hypothesis: The adoption of artificial intelligence is expected to influence the labor market differently depending on the regional concentration of technology jobs. Provinces with more AI-related positions may experience faster wage growth compared to provinces with smaller tech sectors.

Problem Statement

We seek to answer the following questions:

  • How have wages in AI-intensive occupations changed in Canadian provinces between 2012 and 2024?
  • Are urban provinces with a higher concentration of AI-related jobs experiencing faster wage growth than rural provinces, potentially creating a wage gap?

Significance: Understanding these patterns helps policymakers and institutions design strategies to reduce regional wage disparities, support equitable workforce development, and ensure inclusive adaptation to AI-driven labor market changes.

Getting started with the Canadian Wage data

Data Context:

The analysis uses ten provincial wage datasets spanning 2012–2025, with a focus on AI-related occupations.

Key variables include:

  • Year: Tracks changes in wages over time.
  • Average wage (CAD): Measures median hourly wages for technology occupations.
  • Province: Identifies the region, grouped into urban and rural categories.

Occupations chosen are mainly from these categories:

  • Information Technology Systems (example: database administrators, system managers)
  • Data Software and Web Development (example: data scientists, web developers)
  • Engineering and Technical Support Fields (example: civil engineers, data entry clerks)

    • These variables provide a clear view of how AI-related occupations affect wages across regions and occupation types.

Pre-processing data to simplify analysis moving forward

Data Cleaning and Processing:

  1. Standardization: Column names, province abbreviations, and NOC codes were standardized across all datasets. NOC codes from 2021 were mapped back to 2016 equivalents to ensure consistency.
  2. Selection: Only AI-related NOC codes relevant to the research were retained.
  3. Handling Missing Data: Missing values were removed under the assumption that no wage data was recorded for that year or occupation.
  4. Merging: All cleaned datasets were combined into a unified dataset for 2012–2025.
  5. Classification: Provinces were classified as urban or rural to enable comparative analysis.
  6. Calculation of Averages: For each province and year, the average median wage per hour was calculated.

  • Overall, the results suggest that urban and rural wage dynamics differ, supporting the hypothesis that AI-related wage growth contributes to an increasing urban-rural wage gap.
